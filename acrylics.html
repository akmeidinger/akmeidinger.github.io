Acrylic paint is easily one of the widest‑ranging mediums I’ve experimented with. Depending on the type of paint, it can dry at its normal speed or at a slower rate if it’s formulated for extended working time. Acrylics also come with different textures, allowing for more buildable work in paintings or palette‑knife techniques. I often opt for a medium‑body acrylic, which lets me build texture when I want it or create smooth, nearly flat gradients when I don’t. Layering the paint is my most common approach, but palette‑knife painting is fun when I want to loosen up and see where the art takes me. Acrylic paint comes in many forms. Heavy‑body acrylics are thick and often used with palette‑knife work, holding their shape well. Soft‑body acrylics are smoother and more fluid, making them great for blending and detailed brushwork. Fluid acrylics are even thinner and are used for very smooth pieces, pouring techniques, or dripping effects.

Tips & Tricks

  • Use thicker paint for texture.
  • Layer after the paint dries.
  • Have fun with palette knives!
  • Keep your paint moist to keep it useable longer on a palette.
  • Avoid overhead fans if you want the paint to dry slower.
